Building the tools
behind the storefront.

Modernizing Easy USA meant looking beyond the website. I planned, designed, and built a suite of internal tools around how the business actually works—from customer records and wholesale catalogs to sales reporting and warehouse information.

My role

From noticing the problem to putting the tool in people’s hands.

I identified the gaps, mapped the workflows, made the design choices, and tested the tools with the work they needed to support. AI-assisted development helped me carry that work through to implementation.

The suite brings together customer and product tools built into our Shopify admin app, plus connected dashboards and reference material. It gives the team a practical way into the systems behind the business.

A new storefront was only part of the change

Important work still lived across an older accounting system, separate inventory records, manual reports, and one-off documents. Moving the store to Shopify created an opportunity to make those everyday tasks easier too.

I approached this as a series of connected improvements. Some needed a better interface. Others needed a repeatable process, a shared source of information, or documentation someone else could follow.

Familiar where it helps, different where it matters

For customer maintenance, I deliberately kept the familiar Sage-style layout. Staff can compare the daily Sage snapshot with the Shopify record, see differences, and update Shopify without learning an entirely new way to find account details. Sage remains read-only in this screen.

Product Intelligence takes a different approach: it puts wholesale case information first. Case quantities, dimensions, weights, and variant details are easier to work with together. Missing or inconsistent information stays visible, because reliable catalogs and freight calculations depend on getting those facts right.

From a Photoshop task to a repeatable sales workflow

Custom catalogs used to be assembled manually in Photoshop. Now we select a Shopify collection and generate a catalog with product information and links to the website. Layout, pricing, branding, and reseller options make the same tool useful for different buyers.

I connected that work with the AI sales agent, so outreach to different buyer categories can include a relevant catalog. The value is in the connection: merchandising a collection also prepares material the sales workflow can use.

BUILDING FOR CONTINUITY

Make the work easier to carry forward

The suite includes a developer site manual and employee training, because the build is only useful if people can understand and maintain it. The office network map follows the same idea: make information shared, visible, and easier to update.

Some groundwork is still ahead of everyday use. The pallet tools are built, with rollout on hold while product measurements are made reliable. The second warehouse remains a separate ledger; integrating it properly with Shopify Locations is the next direction.

What this adds up to

Catalog creation is now repeatable. Customer records are easier to compare. Sales reporting is automated, and staff have a shared home for tools and guidance. Each improvement removes a little more dependence on a manual task or knowledge held by one person—and gives the business something it can build on.
